Lockout / tagout instructions, by NFC

The shutdown steps, in the language you read.

LockTalk puts machine-specific lockout/tagout instructions on an NFC tag fixed to the machine. A technician taps a phone to the tag and reads the approved procedure in their own language. If something is unclear, a live two-way translation connects them with the person on site.

    How it works

    Tap, read, act. Approve once, update anytime.

    Two sides of one tag: what the technician does at the machine, and what the machine owner does before the tag goes live.

    At the machine.

    The tag sits where the work starts. The instructions are always the ones for that exact machine.

    1. Tag on the machine

      An NFC tag is fixed to the machine and linked to its lockout/tagout procedure.

    2. Tap your phone

      No searching through folders. The phone opens the procedure for the machine in front of you.

    3. Read it in your language

      Each step appears in the technician's own language, in the same order as the original.

    Behind the tag.

    A procedure only reaches technicians after the owner has approved every language. Select a stage to see what is visible and to whom.

    When a procedure changes.

    The owner edits the procedure and approves the new wording. The tag on the machine stays the same, and the next tap shows the new version. Nothing is reprinted.

    What it does

    Two ways to make sure it is understood.

    One covers the written procedure. The other covers the questions that come up at the machine.

    APPROVED TRANSLATION

    Reviewed wording for every step

    AI produces the translation, and the machine owner approves it before it goes live. Technicians always see the approved text, never an unchecked one.

    The translated procedure keeps the order and numbering of the original, so a supervisor and a technician can point at "step 4" and mean the same thing.

    LIVE TWO-WAY TRANSLATION

    Ask a question, get an answer

    When a step is unclear, the technician and the supervisor talk to each other, each in their own language. The original wording stays visible next to the translation.

    What LockTalk is not.

    It does not replace training, permits or your own safety rules. It shows the procedure your organisation has already approved, in the language the reader needs.

    Who it is for

    Built for manufacturing floors with mixed crews.

    LockTalk is for the people responsible for safe shutdowns and the people who carry them out.

    Owns the procedure

    Safety and maintenance leads

    Keep one current procedure per machine and approve the wording once.

    • One procedure per machine, one place to update it
    • Approve each language before it is visible
    • Know what every visitor sees
    RUNS THE FLOOR

    Plant and production managers

    Bring in contractors and agency staff without a briefing in three languages before each job.

    • Faster, clearer start of external jobs
    • Fewer questions relayed through colleagues
    • A consistent way to handle every machine
    Does the work

    International technicians

    Understand the procedure on the first read, and have a way to ask when you are not sure.

    • Steps in your own language
    • Same order and numbering as the original
    • Live help when a step is unclear

    Moments it is made for.

    Three everyday situations where a poster in one language is not enough.

    A new contractor on Monday

    The crew arrives, the machine is in the plant's language, and nobody has time for a full walkthrough. The technician taps the tag and reads the steps.

    A service call outside office hours

    A service engineer from another country needs to isolate a machine. The tag shows the current procedure, and live translation reaches the on-site contact.

    A machine that was modified

    After a change, the procedure is updated and approved. The next person to tap the tag reads the new steps, not an old printout.
